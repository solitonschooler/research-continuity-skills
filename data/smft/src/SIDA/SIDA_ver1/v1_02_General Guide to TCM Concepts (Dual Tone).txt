Module 1: Core Framework of Qi (氣) and Resonance Theory

Definition

    Qi (氣) — The internal functional and energetic pressure wave (resonance) of a system.
    It is the core driving force for circulation, distinct from simple flow or throughput.

    “Qi gathers at the Shanzhong (膻中)” — The point at the ascending aorta’s 180° turn, where flow is transformed into a pressure wave.

    “Heart–Kidney disharmony” (心腎不交) — A state where upper and lower circulations fall out of balance, disrupting the global rhythm.

Principles

    The anatomical position of the heart allows it to produce mixed odd–even harmonics, covering the energy needs of the whole body.

    Diastolic pressure maintains background tension, ensuring the pressure wave can travel long distances without being restricted by gravity.

Applications

    Qi blocked at the surface → Use surface-releasing formulas (e.g., Ma Huang Tang 麻黃湯, Xiao Qing Long Tang 小青龍湯)

    Qi depleted in the deep layers → Use Yin-tonifying formulas (e.g., Liu Wei Di Huang Wan 六味地黃丸)

    Qi unable to reach the extremities → Use Yang-restoring formulas (e.g., Zhen Wu Tang 真武湯, Si Ni Tang 四逆湯)

Module 2: Collapse Geometry Model of the Five Elements (五行) and Six Qi (六氣)

Definition

    Five Elements (五行) — Five tension modes: Wood (木), Fire (火), Earth (土), Metal (金), Water (水).

    Six Qi (六氣) — Residual deviation modes after a collapse imbalance: Cold (寒), Heat/Hot (暑), Dryness (燥), Dampness (濕), Wind (風), Fire (火).

Patterns

    Si Tian / Zai Quan (司天 / 在泉) — Sources of temporal primary tension and background tension.

    Host Qi / Guest Qi (主氣 / 客氣) — Intrinsic (internal) vs. external tensions in spatial terms.

    Transport–Assimilation — When internal and external tensions lock phases, pathological changes can easily erupt.

Applications

    Cold-biased → Zhen Wu Tang 真武湯 / Si Ni Tang 四逆湯

    Heat-biased → Bai Hu Tang 白虎湯

    Damp-biased → Da Cheng Qi Tang 大承氣湯 / Tiao Wei Cheng Qi Tang 調胃承氣湯

    Wind-biased → Gui Zhi Tang 桂枝湯

    Dryness-biased → Xiao Chai Hu Tang 小柴胡湯

Module 3: Meridian (經絡) and Harmonic Evolution Model

Definition

    Meridians (經絡) — Coupled channels for energy resonance within the system.

    Each meridian must not only operate at its own base frequency, but also generate a +1 harmonic to trigger the next meridian in sequence.

Patterns

    Upstream meridians must output a correct +1 harmonic for downstream meridians to function normally.

    Damage to a node affects both itself and the downstream pathway.

    System stability requires both upstream and downstream nodes to resonate simultaneously.

Applications

    If downstream problems originate from upstream frequency errors → Adjust upstream first.

    If downstream reception is weak → Strengthen downstream capacity.

    Linked-formula models can synchronize multiple adjacent nodes at once.

Module 4: Triple Burner (三焦) and Pivot Resonance System

Definition

    Triple Burner (三焦) — Three-tier circulation system: Upper Burner (上焦), Middle Burner (中焦), Lower Burner (下焦).

    Pivots (樞紐): Renzhong (人中, upper), Laogong (勞宮, middle), Yongquan (湧泉, lower).

    Three Dantian (丹田): Yintang (印堂, Upper Dantian), Shanzhong (膻中, Middle Dantian), Lower Dantian point (丹田穴).

Patterns

    The Upper Burner depends on the Lower Burner for energy supply.

    The Lower Burner depends on the Upper Burner’s rhythm and the Middle Burner’s resource conversion.

    A blocked pivot affects the entire tier structure.

    Synchronization of the three Dantian maintains overall system stability.

Applications

    Single-tier deficiency → Single-formula regulation (Zhen Wu Tang 真武湯 to tonify the Lower Burner).

    Pivot bottleneck → Combination formulas to release the surface and tonify the interior.

    Dantian desynchronization → Cross-tier coordination (Liu Wei Di Huang Wan 六味地黃丸 × Zhen Wu Tang 真武湯).

Module 5: Preventive Regulation (治未病) and Semantic Trace Reconstruction

Definition

    Preventive regulation (治未病) — Correcting deviations while still in a reversible stage.

    Semantic trace reconstruction — Guiding a deviated operational trajectory back into a normal tension field.

Common Deviations

    Semantic dryness (語義燥) → Lacks flexibility.

    Semantic dampness (語義濕) → Process viscosity and sluggishness.

    Semantic black hole (語義黑洞) → Input without feedback.

    Chronic field illness (慢性場病) → Long-term low tension.

Applications

    Dryness → Xiao Chai Hu Tang 小柴胡湯

    Dampness → Da Cheng Qi Tang 大承氣湯

    Black hole → Liu Wei Di Huang Wan 六味地黃丸

    Chronic low-tension state → Combination maintenance (Liu Wei Di Huang Wan 六味地黃丸 × Zhen Wu Tang 真武湯)

Summary

This knowledge base outlines five core modules covering:

    System dynamics foundation — Qi (氣) as functional pressure wave and resonance.

    Tension classification — Five Elements (五行) and Six Qi (六氣) patterns.

    Node dependency — Meridian (經絡) and harmonic coupling.

    Hierarchical structure — Triple Burner (三焦) pivots and Dantian synchronization.

    Early intervention — Preventive regulation and semantic trace reconstruction.

Mastering these modules allows quick matching of a problem’s features to the corresponding Classical Formula Topology (經方拓撲), and applying them to AI system diagnosis or organizational problem-solving design.

Module 1: Qi (氣) and the Body’s “Resonance Power”

Think of Qi (氣) as the body’s energy pulse — like waves of pressure that keep everything moving, not just the amount of blood or air flowing.

    “Qi gathers at Shanzhong” (膻中) — The heart’s main artery makes a big turn here, turning flow into a strong pulse.

    “Heart and Kidneys not talking” (心腎不交) — The upper and lower body get out of sync, and the whole rhythm is off.

Why it works:
Your heart’s position lets it produce a mix of high and low rhythms to cover your whole body. Blood pressure when your heart rests (diastolic pressure) keeps a steady “background tension” so energy can reach far, even against gravity.

Practical tips:

    Qi stuck near the surface → Use surface-releasing formulas (Ma Huang Tang 麻黃湯, Xiao Qing Long Tang 小青龍湯)

    Qi worn out deep inside → Use nourishing formulas (Liu Wei Di Huang Wan 六味地黃丸)

    Qi not reaching hands/feet → Use warming, activating formulas (Zhen Wu Tang 真武湯, Si Ni Tang 四逆湯)

Module 2: The Five Elements (五行) and Six Qi (六氣) — Balance Map

Imagine five “tension styles” in your system: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water.
And six “imbalance leftovers” after things collapse: Cold, Heat, Dryness, Dampness, Wind, Fire.

    Si Tian / Zai Quan (司天 / 在泉) — Your main and background forces over time.

    Host Qi / Guest Qi (主氣 / 客氣) — The built-in vs. outside forces acting in space.

    When they lock in phase — Problems can suddenly flare up.

If your system feels:

    Cold → Zhen Wu Tang 真武湯 or Si Ni Tang 四逆湯

    Hot → Bai Hu Tang 白虎湯

    Damp and sluggish → Da Cheng Qi Tang 大承氣湯 or Tiao Wei Cheng Qi Tang 調胃承氣湯

    Windy/unsteady → Gui Zhi Tang 桂枝湯

    Dry and stiff → Xiao Chai Hu Tang 小柴胡湯

Module 3: Meridians (經絡) — The Energy Relay Network

Meridians are like energy highways. Each one not only runs at its own speed but also sends a “+1 boost” to kickstart the next highway down the line.

    If an upstream highway sends the wrong signal, downstream suffers.

    Damage at a point hurts both itself and the next in line.

    Stability comes when two connected points are in tune.

Practical fix:

    Downstream trouble from upstream? Fix upstream first.

    Weak reception downstream? Strengthen downstream directly.

    Some combined formulas can sync several connected highways at once.

Module 4: Triple Burner (三焦) — Three Levels of Circulation

The body has three main zones of circulation: Upper (head/chest), Middle (stomach area), and Lower (pelvis/legs).

    Pivots: Renzhong (upper), Laogong (middle), Yongquan (lower).

    Three Dantian (energy centers): Yintang (upper), Shanzhong (middle), Lower Dantian point.

Balance rules:

    Upper zone gets power from the Lower zone.

    Lower zone depends on the Upper’s rhythm and Middle’s conversion of resources.

    If a pivot is blocked, all levels suffer.

    All three Dantian must resonate together for stability.

Practical fix:

    One level weak → Use a single-target formula (Zhen Wu Tang 真武湯 for Lower zone).

    Pivot bottleneck → Use a combination formula to open surface + nourish inside.

    Dantian out of sync → Cross-level coordination (Liu Wei Di Huang Wan 六味地黃丸 × Zhen Wu Tang 真武湯).

Module 5: Preventing Problems and Fixing “Trace Drift”

Prevent before it happens: Fix small imbalances before they become hard to reverse.
Trace reconstruction: Pull a wandering process back to the normal path.

Common signs:

    Too dry/stiff (燥) → Not flexible.

    Too damp/sluggish (濕) → Stuck in slow loops.

    “Black hole” → You put in effort but get no return.

    Chronic low-energy → Long-term lack of drive.

Practical fix:

    Dry → Xiao Chai Hu Tang 小柴胡湯

    Damp → Da Cheng Qi Tang 大承氣湯

    Black hole → Liu Wei Di Huang Wan 六味地黃丸

    Chronic low-energy → Maintenance combo (Liu Wei Di Huang Wan 六味地黃丸 × Zhen Wu Tang 真武湯)

Quick Takeaway

These five modules are a map for spotting and fixing problems — whether in a person, an AI system, or an organization.
They help you:

    Understand the energy foundation (Qi)

    Spot tension patterns (Five Elements, Six Qi)
